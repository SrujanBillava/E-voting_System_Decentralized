# VoteChain V3: smart-contract integration layer

The on-chain half of Privacy V3: anonymous, encrypted, publicly auditable ballots. It wraps the frozen crypto core in
[`../privacy-v3`](../privacy-v3) (Semaphore V4 membership, BabyJubJub ElGamal, a Groth16 ballot-validity proof) and is
**isolated** from V2: `../smart-contract/` (tag `v2.0.0`, `Voting.sol`) is untouched and shares nothing with this project.

Status: contract core (frozen on `feature/privacy-v3`) plus the **threshold-tally integration** with [`../trustee-v3`](../trustee-v3). Local network only,
**final prototype / research proving setup** (not an independently governed ceremony), not audited; see [Accepted decisions](#accepted-decisions) for what must change before a final V3 release. The contract
accepts ballots and keeps the encrypted sum; after Closed it **pins the trustee ceremony, anchors the trustees' partial-decryption bundles, and finalizes a
constituency when two distinct trustees endorse the same result**. It never decrypts and never verifies a Chaum-Pedersen proof (off-chain auditors do).

## Contract architecture

* **Lifecycle** `Setup -> Open -> Closed`, no reopen. In Setup the owner sets the issuer, the election key `H`, the constituencies and candidates, then
  `openElection()` (needs issuer, key, at least one constituency and at least one candidate in every constituency). Closing is
  `closeIssuance()`, then at least `CLOSE_GRACE` seconds (constructor argument), then `closeElection()`. Ballots are accepted from Open until Closed, so
  voters who already hold a credential can still vote during the grace period. `Ownable2Step`; `renounceOwnership` is disabled so the election can always be closed.
* **Constituencies** are `keccak256(bytes(code))` as in V2, each with a candidate list (K_c = 1..16, slot j = position j of the one-hot vote), an issuance cap
  (`registeredVoters`, fixed in Setup, at most 2^20) and **its own official Semaphore group**.
* **Issuance** `registerCommitmentBatch(constituencyId, commitments[])`: issuer only, Open and issuance open, non-empty, at most `MAX_BATCH` (128),
  every commitment a field element in `1..p-1` and unique **election-wide**, at most one batch per constituency per 30 s epoch (`block.timestamp / 30`),
  never beyond the cap. Commitments enter Semaphore through `addMembers` only.
* **Ballots** `submitBallot(constituencyId, membership, coords, validity)` is **permissionless**: a relayer, the voter or anybody. The caller supplies the constituency,
  a Semaphore proof, the ciphertext coordinates of the *active* slots (4 per candidate, slot-major `C1.x, C1.y, C2.x, C2.y`) and the Groth16 proof.
  The contract itself fixes the scope, the chain id, its own address, the election id, `H`, `K_c`, the group and the ballot hash.
* **Encrypted aggregate** per candidate slot: `A = sum of C1`, `B = sum of C2`, starting at the identity `(0, 1)` (not `(0, 0)`). Only point additions run on-chain.
  `aggregateOf(constituencyId, slot)` exposes it; nothing is decrypted.
* **Audit**: every accepted ballot emits `BallotRecorded(constituencyId indexed, nullifier indexed, ballotIndex indexed, ballotHash, coords[])`. Replaying the events
  off-chain reproduces every aggregate exactly (tested). No log or storage slot holds a voter identity, a commitment-to-ballot mapping, a choice or randomness.

### Trustees and the tally (Closed)

* **`configureTrustees(transcriptHash, trustees[3], verificationKeys[3], H)`** (owner, Setup only): exactly three trustees (threshold 2 is fixed by the design and by the encodings), distinct non-zero addresses,
  a non-zero transcript hash, `H` equal to the registered election key, each `vk_i` canonical, on-curve and non-identity, and mutually consistent with `H` by **point additions only**
  (`H = 2 vk_1 - vk_2`, `vk_3 = 2 vk_2 - vk_1`, `vk_1 != vk_2`, the last so that no constant sharing polynomial, i.e. a trustee holding the whole secret, can be pinned). Setting another election key clears it;
  `openElection` reverts `TrusteesNotConfigured`; it is immutable once Open. Subgroup membership stays an off-chain ceremony duty (no scalar multiplication on-chain).
* **`publishPartialDecryption(constituencyId, trusteeIndex, bundle[64])`** (Closed): `msg.sender` must be the address pinned for `trusteeIndex`, once per trustee per constituency, never replaced, the constituency must hold ballots.
  The bundle is 16 slots x `(D.x, D.y, e, z)`; the contract checks the **shape** only (active slots: `D` on the curve, `0 < e, z < l`; padded slots: four zero words), computes the bundle hash **itself**
  (`V3Encodings.partialBundleHash`: tag `keccak256("VOTECHAIN-V3-PDEC-BUNDLE-1")`, chain id, contract, election id, transcript hash, trustee index, constituency, ballot count, `K_c`, the 64 words), stores **only the hash**
  and emits `PartialDecryptionPublished(..., bundleHash, ballotCount, candidateCount, words[])` with the active words, enough for anybody to rebuild and verify the bundle.
* **`endorseResult(constituencyId, trusteeIndex, totals[16])`** (Closed): the pinned trustee, who must have published (a constituency without ballots needs none: its only valid result is zeros); exactly `K_c` active totals,
  each `<=` the ballot count, padded totals zero, `sum == ballotCount`. The contract computes the results hash itself (`V3Encodings.resultsHash`, tag `keccak256("VOTECHAIN-V3-RESULTS-1")`) and stores it per trustee, once, never
  replaced. When **two distinct trustees endorsed the same hash** the constituency is finalized, immutably (`ConstituencyFinalized`); disagreeing trustees finalize nothing and there is no tie-break.
* **Views**: `trusteeConfiguration()`, `partialBundleHash`, `endorsementOf`, `isFinalized`, and `finalResult(constituencyId)`, which **reverts `NotFinalized` until finalization** (nothing is readable before). There is no function that decrypts
  or exposes an individual ballot, and an ABI test pins the complete list of names that mention decryption, results or trustees.
* **Off-chain**, `trustee-v3` rebuilds each constituency's aggregate from the complete `BallotRecorded` log and compares it with `aggregateOf` before any trustee decrypts; the auditor verifies every Chaum-Pedersen proof, combines two partials,
  recovers the totals by BSGS and checks them against the finalized result (see [`../trustee-v3/README.md`](../trustee-v3/README.md#tallying-against-votechainv3)).

### `submitBallot` check order (nothing is written before every check has passed)

1 phase is Open, 2 constituency exists, 3 shape (`4*K_c` coordinates, declared depth 20), 4 nullifier is a field element and unused (election-wide),
5 every coordinate is a field element, 6 no active `C1` is the identity, 7 canonical padded `uint256[64]` (padded slots = identity ciphertext `(0,1,0,1)`),
8 frozen `ballotHash`, 9 Semaphore `verifyProof` (this constituency's group, the contract's scope, message = the contract's ballot hash),
10 Groth16 over the 68 public signals `[nullifier, K_c, H.x, H.y, 64 coordinates]` built by the contract, 11 the same nullifier in both proofs (one value feeds both
statements), then 12 mark the nullifier, 13 add into the aggregates, 14 counters, 15 emit the event. The test `the CHECKS RUN IN THE SPECIFIED ORDER` pins the order.

## Semaphore integration

* Official `@semaphore-protocol/contracts` **4.14.3** (`Semaphore.sol`, `SemaphoreVerifier.sol`, `PoseidonT3`), the version `privacy-v3` uses for `group`, `identity` and `proof`.
  The depth-20 circuit artifacts are the pinned Semaphore 4.13.0 set that `@semaphore-protocol/proof` 4.14.3 requests (see `../privacy-v3/README.md`).
* `addConstituency` calls `createGroup(address(this), ROOT_WINDOW)` and asserts `getGroupAdmin(groupId) == address(this)`: **VoteChainV3 is the only group admin**.
  It exposes no `updateGroupAdmin`, `acceptGroupAdmin`, `removeMember`, `updateMember`, `addMember(s)` pass-through or `updateGroupMerkleTreeDuration`; `registerCommitmentBatch` is the only path
  to `addMembers`. Tests prove that owner, issuer, deployer and strangers are all refused by Semaphore itself.
* Ballots use **`verifyProof`, never `validateProof`** (Semaphore's own nullifier store is per group; V3 needs one election-wide nullifier set, kept by VoteChainV3).
  `verifyProof` reverts for an unknown or expired root and for an empty group, and returns `false` for a bad proof (VoteChainV3 reverts `InvalidMembershipProof`).
* Scope = `uint256(keccak256(abi.encode(SCOPE_TAG, chainId, address(this), ELECTION_ID)))`, message = the ballot hash; both computed by the contract.
* **Root window**: `merkleTreeDuration = 1 hours`. Semaphore measures it from the **creation of the root being used**, so a proof against a superseded root is valid until one hour
  after that root was created; the current root never expires. With at most one batch per constituency per 30 s there are at most 120 live roots per hour.
* Depth is fixed at **20**: every proof must declare depth 20 (shorter groups still prove at depth 20), so a constituency holds at most 2^20 = 1,048,576 commitments.

## Validity verifier integration

`BallotValidityVerifier.sol` (`Groth16Verifier`) is generated from `privacy-v3/artifacts/build/ballot_validity_final.zkey` by `snarkjs zkey export solidityverifier`
and never edited. VoteChainV3 builds the 68 signals itself: nullifier from the Semaphore proof, `K_c` and `H` from storage, the padded coordinates from the
submitted slots, so a copied proof, another `K_c`, another key or a vote in a padded slot all fail verification. `ValidityProof.b` is in the order snarkjs' Solidity export
expects (`[[pi_b[0][1], pi_b[0][0]], [pi_b[1][1], pi_b[1][0]]]`). The tests check that the committed verifier is exactly what snarkjs emitted, that its verification key
equals the committed final-ceremony manifest `privacy-v3/spec/final-ceremony.json` (and the build record `results/build-info.json`), that regenerating it from the committed final zkey with the pinned command gives the identical file, and that real proofs (K_c = 2, 8, 16) verify on-chain while a proof from the OLD test setup does not.

The committed verifier is the **FINAL PROTOTYPE / RESEARCH CEREMONY VERIFIER**: it is generated, unmodified, from the zkey of `privacy-v3`'s final prototype ceremony (the unchanged circuit,
public Perpetual Powers of Tau as phase 1, then three phase-2 contributions and a public beacon, `privacy-v3/spec/final-ceremony.json`), and `BallotValidityVerifier.meta.json` says so. That ceremony ran on
**one development machine**: **a production election would require a genuinely independently administered phase 2 and a verifier regenerated from it.** The zkey is committed and cannot be rebuilt;
`npm run build:circuit` in `privacy-v3` only provisions and hash-checks it. A NEW ceremony (`final-ceremony.mjs --redo`) changes the verification key: run `npm run export:verifier` afterwards.

## Running

```bash
(cd ../privacy-v3 && npm ci && npm run build:circuit)   # once: the JS reference core and its proving artifacts
(cd ../trustee-v3 && npm ci)                              # the tally tests import the trustee toolkit (src/, chain/, testing/)
npm ci
npm test                                                  # compile + the whole suite (about 5 min with the real-proof tally test)
```

Without the privacy-v3 artifacts the tests that generate real proofs are reported as pending, not silently dropped. The in-process Hardhat network needs no port.
`world.js` deploys VoteChainV3 as account 0's first transaction so it lands at `0x5FbDB2315678afecb367f032d93F642f64180aa3` on chain 31337: the address and election id of the
first frozen vector, so the deployed contract's `scope()` equals `privacy-v3/spec/vectors.json` exactly.

## Gas (local Hardhat network, hardfork `osaka`, block gas limit 60M, per-transaction cap 2^24 = 16,777,216; `results/gas.json`)

| `submitBallot` | first ballot of a constituency | later ballots | calldata |
|---|---|---|---|
| K_c = 2 | 1,138,360 | about 1,057,400 | 964 B |
| K_c = 8 | 1,477,648 | about 1,273,200 | 1,732 B |
| K_c = 16 | 1,952,716 | about 1,561,000 | 2,756 B |

The two verifiers are fixed costs: Groth16 637,978 and Semaphore `verifyProof` 234,161. What grows with K_c is aggregation (about 36k per extra slot: two additions
at about 6.1k each, four storage writes of 5k, calldata and log data). The first ballot is dearer because the aggregate words go from zero to non-zero.

| `registerCommitmentBatch` | gas | per commitment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 206,077 | 206,077 |
| 16 | 1,472,457 | 92,029 |
| 64 | 5,162,608 | 80,666 |
| 128 (`MAX_BATCH`) | 10,137,345 | 79,198 (60% of the per-tx cap) |

Per commitment: two fresh storage slots (VoteChainV3's uniqueness map and Semaphore's leaf index, about 44k) plus Poseidon hashing in the Semaphore tree (about 30k per hash,
roughly 37% of a batch). A deeper tree adds hashes along the path to the root only (a 128-batch needs 135 hashes at about 600,000 members instead of 127 in an empty tree).
Nothing approaches a limit: the largest transactions are a 128-commitment batch (16.9% of the block limit, 60.4% of the per-tx cap) and a first K_c = 16 ballot (3.3% / 11.6%).
Hardhat's `eth_estimateGas` fails for batches of about 100 or more under Osaka (it probes roughly three times the real usage, above the cap); the transaction itself fits, so
the issuer service must pass an explicit gas limit. Nothing here has been optimised.

## Known limits and decisions

* **Two malicious trustees can still endorse, and so finalize, a false result.** This is an **accepted prototype limitation**: the contract verifies no Chaum-Pedersen proof (that is deliberate, it would be expensive), and only
  checks that endorsed totals are internally consistent (each `<=` ballots, sum `==` ballots). Public auditors can independently verify the trustees' published partials and the decrypted aggregate;
  `trustee-v3`'s `readVerifiedFinalResult` fails with `FINAL_RESULT_MISMATCH` on such a result (demonstrated in `test/tally.test.js`).
* **Finalization gates the official result, not the information.** `finalResult` reverts until two trustees agree, and the contract stores no total before that. But the partial decryptions are public by design, so anybody can
  compute a constituency's totals as soon as two valid bundles are anchored (that is what makes the result publicly verifiable), and an endorsement's calldata carries its totals. The election is Closed by then, so nothing
  is being hidden from voters; no individual ballot is ever exposed.
* A trustee configuration is pinned, not proved: the contract cannot check the DKG, so the transcript hash is the commitment that every trustee and auditor checks off-chain against the published transcript (and `H`, `vk_i`, `{n: 3, t: 2}`).
* **`H` subgroup membership is not checked on-chain** (it needs a scalar multiplication, deliberately absent). `setElectionKey` checks canonical, on-curve and `x != 0`;
  the validation is an off-chain key-ceremony responsibility (decision 4 below). A test records this as a known, documented limit.
* An accepted ballot's coordinates are exactly what the validity proof computed, so the aggregate only ever adds valid subgroup points; the addition is complete for them.
* A constituency is capped at 2^20 voters by the depth-20 tree; `MAX_BATCH = 128` was chosen from the measured gas (decision 1 below).
* `closeIssuance` plus `CLOSE_GRACE` comes from the frozen architecture notes (close sequence `closeIssuance -> grace -> closeElection`); the grace period is a constructor argument.
* The issuer is set in Setup only and immutable after Open (decision 2 below). The nullifier set and the commitment set are election-wide.
* Duplicate-ciphertext detection is left to the auditor, as frozen.

## Accepted decisions

Decisions 1-4 were accepted when the contract core was frozen, 5-6 with the tally integration. Do not change them without a new decision.

1. **`MAX_BATCH = 128`.** Chosen from the measured gas results (a 128-commitment batch costs 10,137,345 gas, 60.4% of the 2^24 per-transaction cap of the local Osaka network; see
   the gas table). It may be changed **only if a later target chain requires a lower limit** (a smaller per-transaction gas cap or block gas limit).
2. **The issuer is Setup-only and immutable after Open.** `setIssuer` works only in Setup; there is **no issuer rotation during Open** and none will be added. If issuer compromise is
   suspected during Open, the operational response is **`closeIssuance()`**, which stops all further commitment registration at once while voters who already hold a credential can
   still vote until `closeElection()`.
3. **The committed `BallotValidityVerifier` is the final prototype / research ceremony verifier**, not a production-ceremony one. It is generated from the final zkey of `privacy-v3`'s prototype ceremony
   (three contributions and a public beacon, all on one development machine). A production election **MUST use a verifier generated from a genuinely independently administered phase 2.** Its provenance
   stays checked: the tests compare the committed Solidity with the SHA-256 in the final-ceremony manifest, regenerate it from the committed final zkey with the pinned command, and compare its embedded
   verification key with the committed `privacy-v3/spec/verification_key.json`. Regenerate with `npm run export:verifier` after any new ceremony and update the manifest together.
4. **Validating the election public key `H` (prime-order subgroup) is an off-chain key-ceremony responsibility.** The final trustee ceremony must validate `H` (privacy-v3
   `validatePublicKey`) **before it is registered**, and the trustees must **independently confirm the registered key**. No expensive on-chain scalar multiplication will be added just for this
   check; on-chain, `setElectionKey` keeps its cheap canonical, on-curve and `x != 0` checks.

5. **Chaum-Pedersen proofs are verified off-chain** by trustees and auditors; the chain pins the trustee configuration, anchors the published bundles, enforces identity, phase, uniqueness and the two-matching-endorsements rule,
   and stores final results. No on-chain proof verification and no on-chain scalar multiplication (not even for subgroup membership) will be added without a new decision.
6. **Two malicious trustees can finalize a false result** (see Known limits): accepted for the prototype because the auditor can detect it from public data.
